Why Rajasthani menus need their own plan

A Rajasthani spread is not a North Indian buffet with one extra dish. Baati timing, ghee service, spice balance and how guests move through a thali all shape whether the meal feels authentic or merely themed.

Shree Gajanan Caterers builds Rajasthani catering in Jaipur around dishes that hold up at scale — prepared with the same discipline we bring to wedding and outdoor work.

Signature dishes we prepare

  • Dal Bati Churma — baati baked or finished for the counter, dal that stays consistent through service, churma portioned for the line
  • Ker Sangri — the desert classic guests look for on a traditional menu
  • Gatte ki sabzi and other gram-flour preparations that sit well beside richer mains
  • Laal maas or vegetarian alternatives when the celebration mixes preferences
  • Bajra / missi rotis and supporting breads for a fuller thali
  • Rajasthani sweets — ghevar, malpua or seasonal desserts coordinated with the meal

Live baati counters

Live baati draws guests and creates a natural pause in the meal. It also needs heat, space and staffing so the counter does not bottleneck the rest of service.

We plan live baati when the venue can support it — indoor halls with proper kitchen adjacency, or outdoor sites where power and safety are sorted early. Pair it with outdoor catering logistics when the function is on a lawn or farmhouse.

Thali and buffet formats

Some families want a composed Rajasthani thali; others prefer a buffet with regional stations. Both work when guest count and venue layout are clear from the start.

  • Thali-style plating for more formal or compact gatherings
  • Buffet with dedicated Rajasthani counters for larger weddings
  • Mixed menus — Rajasthani core with North Indian or live chaat for variety
  • Vegetarian-forward lines with Jain options on request — see vegetarian catering
