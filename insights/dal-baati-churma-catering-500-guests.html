Dal Baati Churma catering for 500 guests

At five hundred plates, Dal Baati stops being a side dish and becomes a production plan — batches, heat and service style decide the result.

Decide the role of Dal Baati first

Is Dal Baati Churma the main meal, a strong counter among a wider buffet, or a Sawamani-style prasadi centrepiece? That choice changes portion planning. A main-meal approach needs more baati per guest; a counter approach can sit beside rice, sabzi and bread lines without over-ordering.

What breaks at scale

Cold baati, thin dal and dry churma are usually timing problems. Large guest lists need staggered finishing, enough staff to replenish, and a clear decision between pangat seating and free-flow buffet. Ask the caterer how they keep baati textured through the last seating, not only the first.

Questions to ask before you book

  • Approximate baati and dal portions per guest for your service style
  • Whether churma is finished in batches or held too early
  • Supporting dishes that prevent the line from stalling
  • Kitchen footprint for indoor halls versus outdoor venues
  • Staff count for replenishment during peak arrival

Where this fits other menus

Weddings often pair Dal Baati with broader Rajasthani catering. Religious gatherings lean on Sawamani Prashadi catering. For caterer options, see Dal Baati Churma caterers in Jaipur.
